How do you use AI safely when it's just you, or a small team?

Your data, your accounts, what never goes in, checking the output, the scams aimed at small firms, and a one-page AI rule you can print today.

About 11 minutesLast checked November 2026

The short answer

Five things cover most of it: use business accounts whose data terms you've read, keep a short list of what never goes in, lock down the accounts AI touches, check anything that leaves the building, and call back before you pay any urgent or changed request.

Write it on one page. It takes an afternoon, and it's the same afternoon whether you're on your own or have twenty people.

The gap

Why this is worth an afternoon

Small firms have taken to AI fast. In the US Chamber of Commerce's 2025 survey, 58% of small businesses said they use generative AI, up from 23% two years earlier.1 The safeguards haven't kept pace. In the UK government's 2026 cyber security survey, of the businesses using or considering AI, only about a quarter (24%) had any cyber security practices to manage the risks.2

The most common real AI problem in a small business isn't dramatic. It's a well-meaning person pasting a client file into a free chatbot, a confident wrong figure in a quote, or a convincing email asking to change the bank details on an invoice. All three are cheap to prevent and expensive to clean up.

1 · Data

Your data: where it goes

The data terms are the point, not the features.

Whatever you type into an AI tool goes to the company that runs it. What happens next depends on the type of account and its settings, and the difference between personal and business plans is real:

  • OpenAI says it doesn't train on business products (ChatGPT Business, ChatGPT Enterprise and the API) by default. On personal ChatGPT accounts, conversations can be used for training unless you switch off "Improve the model for everyone" in the data settings.3
  • Anthropic changed its consumer terms in 2025: people on its Free, Pro and Max plans now choose whether their chats are used for training, and if they allow it, data can be kept for up to five years. Its business and API products are excluded.4

Other providers have their own rules, and all of them change. The cautionary tale is old but still the clearest: in 2023, Samsung engineers uploaded internal source code to ChatGPT, and the company temporarily banned generative AI tools on its devices while it reviewed security.5 Samsung could afford the lesson. Most small firms would rather skip it.

Customer data is still customer data. Under the GDPR, "processing" covers almost anything you do with personal data, including retrieving, using and disclosing it.6 Putting a customer's details into an AI tool counts. Similar rules apply in many other countries. If you wouldn't email it to a stranger, don't paste it into a tool you haven't checked.

2 · The never-list

What never goes in

A short list people can remember beats a long policy nobody reads. Start with this, then adjust for your trade.

Never, in any AI tool
  • Passwords, PINs, recovery codes, API keys
  • Full bank, card or payment details
  • ID documents: passports, driver's licenses, tax or social security numbers
Only in an approved business account, if at all
  • Customer or client personal details
  • Health, HR, disciplinary or payroll information
  • Anything under an NDA or a client confidentiality clause
  • Unreleased prices, bids, accounts or plans
  • Legal advice and live disputes

The habit that makes most of this easy: swap real details for placeholders. "Client A," "[amount]," "[address]." The AI does the same job on the wording, and you fill the details back in yourself.

3 · Accounts

Your accounts and connected tools

An AI account connected to your email is a key to your business.

One person, one account
No shared logins. Business accounts let you see who has access and remove it.
Turn on two-step login
On email first, then on every AI tool. Use passkeys or an authenticator app where you can.
Remove leavers the same day
Including freelancers and contractors. Their chat history may hold your files.
Be careful what you connect
AI tools that read your email, files or calendar, or act on your behalf, can be tricked by instructions hidden in a web page, document or email. This is called prompt injection, and the UK's National Cyber Security Centre warned in December 2025 that it "may never be totally mitigated."7 Give connected tools the least access that does the job.
Nothing irreversible without you
No AI tool sends payments, deletes records or emails customers on its own. It can draft; you press the button.

The basics underneath all of this, backups, device updates and spotting scams, are in the NCSC's free small organisations guide.8 Our digital resilience checklist covers the same ground in ten minutes.

4 · Output

Checking what comes out

AI tools are confident when they're wrong. In a small business there's often no second person to catch it, so the check has to be a habit:

  • Anything a customer sees gets read by a person first: quotes, emails, web copy, social posts, chatbot answers.
  • Every fact, figure, price, date and name gets checked against a source you opened yourself.
  • Legal, tax, health and safety wording gets checked with someone qualified, not with another chatbot.
  • If you run a chatbot on your website, assume what it promises, you promised. A Canadian tribunal held an airline to exactly that.

5 · Scams

Scams aimed at small firms

Voice clones, urgent transfers, fake tools and the call-back rule.

"It's me, I need this paid today"

Fake emails from a boss, supplier or client asking for a payment or new bank details remain one of the costliest crimes reported to the FBI: victims reported losses of more than US$3 billion in 2025. The FBI now flags AI as one way these messages get written, and reported losses from cases involving AI passed US$30 million.9 The technique scales up as well as down. In 2024, engineering firm Arup confirmed that an employee in Hong Kong paid out HK$200 million (about US$25 million) after a video call on which the "chief financial officer" and colleagues were all deepfakes.10 Phishing is still the most common attack overall: in the UK survey, 38% of businesses had experienced it in the past year.2

The call-back rule stops most of it: any urgent payment, any change of bank details, any request for passwords or codes gets a call back on a number you already had, not one in the message. A video call or a familiar voice doesn't count as proof. Agree a code word with anyone who can move money.

The fake AI tool

Criminals advertise "free AI" tools that are really malware. In 2025, Google's Mandiant team found more than 30 fake AI video-generator websites promoted through thousands of ads on Facebook and LinkedIn; the downloads stole logins, browser cookies and card details.11 Get AI tools from the provider's own website or an official app store, and be suspicious of any "free full version."

"Our AI will run your online store"

Some schemes sell AI as the business itself. In 2024 the US Federal Trade Commission went after several, alleging that one, Ascend Ecom, took at least US$25 million from people with promises that its "AI-powered" tools would bring passive income from online stores.12 If an offer promises guaranteed income because of AI, treat it as a sales pitch until proven otherwise.

The template

Write the one-page rule

Fill this in, print it, and walk everyone through it once with real examples. For a sole trader, it's a note to yourself and an answer ready for the client who asks, "Do you use AI on my information?"

Our AI rule

How we use AI at

Tools we use
on business accounts only, with training on our data switched off. Nothing else for client work without asking .
Never goes in
Passwords and codes, bank and card details, ID documents. Customer, health or HR details only in the approved account, and only when needed. Use placeholders.
Always checked by a person
Anything a customer, supplier or the public sees. Every fact, figure, price and name.
A person decides
Payments, refunds, prices, hiring, legal or health matters, deleting records. AI can draft; a person decides.
Money rule
Any urgent payment or change of bank details: call back on a number we already had. Code word: agreed in person.
Accounts
One person, one login, two-step login on. Leavers removed the same day.
If something goes wrong
Tell within the hour. No blame for reporting it.
Reviewed
, and again whenever we add a tool or the terms change.
Prints the template only, with blanks to fill in.

Working with customers in the EU? The EU AI Act asks organizations that use AI to take measures to support their staff's AI literacy (Article 4). A 2026 amendment softened the wording, so no particular level has to be guaranteed, but the duty remains.13 A one-page rule plus a short walk-through is a sensible start. Actions

What you can do

This week
  • Run the ten-minute inventory. Sole trader: list every AI tool you've used for work this month. Team: send one message asking everyone to reply with theirs, including free ones. Promise no consequences.
  • Fix the settings. For each tool, check the data controls and switch off training on your content, or move to a business plan.
  • Turn on two-step login for email and every AI account.
  • Agree the call-back rule and a code word with anyone who can move money.
